ANRF AISEHack 2.0 · Round 2 · Competitive teardown

The Sokhda Six

Six teams were shortlisted for measuring the same 966 farms from the same four Capella scenes. Reading the other five surfaced a defect in our own radiometry, a flaw in how we weight our health index, and — measured against a competitor's published labels — all five competitors' actual submissions measured against ours.

Team GDHTM 29 August 2026 5 competitor writeups · all figures recomputed from shipped artefacts

Headline: our radiometric calibration is wrong — and it barely matters

Two rival teams form brightness as β₀ = SF²·|z|². We use SF·|z|². Measured on our own rasters, our shipped γ⁰ sits at +7.2 to +9.3 dB — a physical impossibility for a farm field.

Rebuilding the entire submission with the correction changes 8 of 966 crop labels, gives Spearman 0.9908 on health, and moves village production by 0.5%. Every shipped quantity is a difference between dates or a rank within a crop — never an absolute level — and that discipline is exactly what contained our own error.

01The calibration finding

This is not an adoption suggestion. It is a defect, found by reading two competitors and then measuring our own data.

What each team ships

Coding Bits use σ⁰ = (SF·|A|)²·sinθ. They state they tested our convention explicitly — "the scale factor applies to power rather than amplitude" — and rejected it, because it places the scene maximum at +46.9 dB, unattainable for a distributed target.

Project Orion use β₀ = |I+jQ|²·SF², citing Capella's own reference implementation (capella-reader, rtc_isce3.py: beta0_complex = SF · DN). A complex amplitude scaled by SF gives power scaled by SF².

We use β₀ = SF·|z|², with a code comment asserting that squaring the scale factor was a Round 1 bug. Our report then concedes γ⁰ reads "about +17 dB above the physically expected level" and blames Capella's undeclared absolute accuracy.

Three lines of evidence, all converging

  1. Physics. A median field γ⁰ of +7 to +9 dB is impossible for a distributed target. Under SF² we land at −18 to −20 dB, the textbook range.
  2. Two competitors' measured values. Coding Bits −20.2 dB, Orion −21.5 dB. We land at −18.2 to −19.8 dB under the squared convention.
  3. Their falsification test reproduces on our data exactly. Coding Bits report the rejected convention giving a +46.9 dB scene max. Ours, using precisely that convention, gives +41.7 to +49.5 dB. The 26.7 dB gap between conventions is exactly 10·log₁₀(SF).

Why we got it wrong — and why that improves the answer

We did not invent the unsquared form. ESA's EDAP Technical Note for Capella Data Assessment (Issue 1.0, p7) states verbatim:

RADAR backscatter: beta0 = sc * |pixel|², where sc is a Scale Factor

Our transcription was accurate. The error is one level down — identifying ESA's sc with the product JSON's collect.image.scale_factor. Capella's own capella-reader settles it: beta0_complex = SF · DN, so power carries SF squared, meaning ESA's sc is the JSON field squared. Both formulas are right; only the substitution was wrong.

If a judge cites ESA at us, the answer is not an admission but a precise technical point.

Corroboration from a third direction

Team oindrieelmondal describe their chain as DN → power → scale_factor → β₀ — our convention — and are the only other team reporting an unexplained absolute offset against Capella's preview product (+1.81, +0.94, +1.57, −2.00 dB). They refuse to call it resolved. Same convention, same symptom.

What it actually costs us

The scale factor is per-scene, not per-pixel, so the error is one constant dB shift applied identically to all 966 farms on a given date. That makes the damage far smaller than the number suggests.

  • Untouched: anything z-scored or ranked across farms within a fixed date — level, growth, uniform, and the within-crop health scoring. The constant cancels.
  • Shifted by a constant: every cross-date difference, again identically for every farm.
  • Genuinely changed: the season integral, which combines dates in linear power. Spearman against the corrected version is 0.9916 (n=913); 124 farms move more than 50 ranks, 11 move more than 100.
  • Void: every absolute-level claim in the report.

03The five teams

Coding Bits — the methodologist

The strongest methodology in the field. Thin-plate spline geocoding over the 225 GCPs (a polynomial left ~8 m residual against a 24.7 m median plot). The Quegan–Yu multitemporal speckle filter, which exploits cross-date correlation instead of spatial averaging, so a 0.4 ha plot keeps its full pixel count — 2.4–2.8× effective looks with zero blurring.

Three things they do that we should copy outright:

  • Spatial hold-out for weight selection. The west half selects, the east half reports. They then reject their own data-driven optimum: the western optimum improves the east by 0.0006 (noise), while the physical prior improves both halves independently. That is how you tell a generalising term from an overfitted one.
  • The informative null. For their radar-only crop map they don't compare against random assignment — the area constraint alone induces a crop/plot-size association. They build the constraint-only null (H=12) and beat it (H=115).
  • The model ceiling, refused. A gradient-boosted regressor beats their unfitted index (0.560 vs 0.462). They don't adopt it: an index optimised to predict NDVI is trainable only where optical data already exists, forfeiting the operational reason to use radar.

They also turn the 19 June rain event into signal rather than discarding it — a closed canopy attenuates the soil contribution, so the magnitude of the rain response is an inverse proxy for canopy cover. Water Cloud Model, run backwards.

Megalodon — the instrument specialist

The best physics, and the single most impressive act of scientific honesty in the set. They retracted their own published finding: they first reported that more uniform fields score higher (ρ = −0.53), exactly what the rubric expects. Then they found the texture had been computed on noise-subtracted intensity, which adds variance in proportion to how near the noise floor a farm sits — dim farms are near the floor and unhealthy, so the statistic was a signal-to-noise proxy wearing an agronomic label. Corrected: ρ = +0.02, and all five structure metrics reverse sign.

"Uniform here means uniformly nothing."

They also measured the auxiliary-data ceiling before fetching anything — a covariate constant within a cell explains at most 0.226 of farm-to-farm variance at 250 m, 0.049 at 1 km, and 0.000 at 50 km. Their validation against Sentinel-1 VH (ρ = 0.746) is the strongest independent check any team reports.

Project Orion — the twin

Two-tier crop labels: rice and cotton from measured physical thresholds, the rest "an allocation on a measured axis, not a classification", every row carrying crop_confidence. Their circularity test is the sharpest instrument in the field — they rank tier 2 on γ⁰, note that NDVI correlates +0.533 with that same axis, and so regress NDVI on γ⁰ first and run the ANOVA on the residual. Tier 2 explains 19% of raw NDVI variance and 0.2% of the residual. Their tier-1 control moves the opposite way, which is what makes the null a measurement rather than a blunt instrument.

8bit — the purist

The only genuinely SAR-only pipeline. Their core idea is elegant: the four scenes span 28.69–35.24° incidence, so rather than correct for it they anchor every temporal metric on the 19 Jun / 14 Aug pair, which is 0.08° apart. No empirical correction needed. Their decile-trend presentation — NDVI rising 0.260 → 0.415 across health deciles, rank correlation +0.988 — is worth copying purely as a way of reporting.

oindrieelmondal — the candid one

Weakest methodology, strongest instinct for reporting its own limits. They state that 50.8% of farms carry crop-type confidence below 0.40 and recommend those for manual review before crop_type is treated as final. They tested a literature claim about erectophile cereals and reported it unsupported — maize's apparent support reversed sign after outlier cleaning.

04Claim collisions

The most valuable content in the set, because all six teams are describing the same 966 farms. Two I resolved on our own data.

Does the rice flood signature work?

Resolved · both sides right

Orion measured the classic dark-flood rule failing: −0.36 dB median where it needs +6 dB. Coding Bits found rice doesn't separate on Aug→Oct retention. But we, Megalodon and oindrieelmondal all use a June flood signature and report it working.

Orion is right about the rule they tested, and it is not the rule we use. The classic signature genuinely fails on our data too (−0.71 dB for rice, wrong sign). Ours is the 6→19 June wetting response, where rice separates from every other crop by 2.1–3.2 dB. Both Coding Bits and Megalodon independently confirm 19 June was a rain day (12.5 mm and 21.9 mm), which supplies the mechanism: a flooded transplanted paddy responds to surface wetting far more strongly than a field already carrying canopy.

Is a uniform field a healthy field?

Resolved · tested, term survives

Our uniform family carries the largest weight in our health index (0.301) and has the same sign as the finding Megalodon publicly retracted. Coding Bits report their uniformity term correlating −0.146 with October greenness — opposite in sign to their other three components — and near-neutral on leave-one-out. Orion side-steps the problem entirely by using CoV excess over the L=1 speckle prediction, which is better posed: at one look, fully developed speckle gives CoV = 1.0 exactly, so any excess is real heterogeneity.

Three of five teams report this term as neutral, negative, or retracted. Our own report already admits it fails on an independent date — so I tested it.

What the test found

We do not have Megalodon's artefact. Theirs was variance rising as a farm nears the noise floor, so dim farms read as non-uniform — ρ(CV, brightness) negative. Ours is +0.173, the opposite sign. But ρ(CV, pixel count) is +0.229, which is the Coding Bits artefact instead: larger plots read as more variable.

The term is null against both witnesses — ρ = −0.030 vs Sentinel-2 NDVI, −0.020 vs Sentinel-1 VH (+0.084 and +0.051 with brightness partialled out).

The real finding is one level up

Our weights are wk ∝ 1/Σj|ρ(k,j)| — each family rewarded for being uncorrelated with the others. The rule is genuinely blind to every witness, which is why our report defends it. But independence is not information, and a family carrying only noise correlates with nothing, so the rule hands it the largest weight.

06Yield: a good measurement on a bad label

One column puts us on the wrong side of the field. My first explanation for it was wrong, and testing it produced a better answer.

Pairwise Spearman on yield puts us negatively correlated with three of five teams (Coding Bits −0.266, 8bit −0.315, Orion −0.313). We are the only team there.

The wrong explanation, tested and rejected

Our bajra anchor is 40–100% above every other team's, so the obvious read was that our inverted per-crop ordering caused it. Correcting bajra to the external median changes the cross-team correlations by a mean of −0.001. Not the cause.

07Testing their claims, not trusting them

Everything above began with something another team asserted. A shortlisted writeup is a hypothesis, not a result — and two of these claims would change numbers we ship.

Calibration, adjudicated by the vendor's own noise floor

The three arguments for the squared convention were all somebody's reading: Coding Bits argued from plausibility, Orion cited capella-reader, ESA states a formula whose sc is ambiguous. So I went to a number Capella itself put in the product: nesz_peak, an absolute dB noise floor, declared per scene.

A real scene contains smooth dark surfaces whose return approaches the system noise floor. The darkest percentiles must sit near it — never tens of dB above, which would mean the sensor never reaches its own noise anywhere.

The bajra anchor: they are right, for a reason none of them gave

Three teams quoting 1.36–1.89 t/ha against our 2.71 is a majority, not evidence. Two independent checks.

Our own table is not internally consistent. Yield should equal production over area. Bajra's implied value is 2,478 kg/ha against the 2,714 we ship — the largest gap of the four non-cotton crops, in the direction that inflates our anchor. A 9% cut before any competitor number is consulted.

And the remaining gap has a mechanism. Gujarat grows bajra in two non-comparable seasons:

Our anchor is an annual district figure — every season summed — and it lands exactly where a blend of the two should. Every competitor figure is explicitly kharif, and all three sit inside the rainfed kharif band. Round 2 observes 6 June to 13 October. That is kharif.

So A5 is not "copy their bajra number". It is: source a kharif anchor for every crop, and check each crop for a second cropping season before using an annual district mean. That rule catches bajra, flags maize, and comes from agronomy rather than a competitor's table.

One of their ideas would have broken our pipeline

Orion re-poses uniformity as CoV excess over the L=1 speckle prediction — at one look, fully developed speckle gives CoV = 1.0 exactly. Better posed than raw CoV, and I had it in the backlog as A3.

It does not port. Our prep_r2 resamples by average onto a 5 m grid, which multilooks. Measured on our own farms, median CoV is 0.43–0.49 — 4 to 5 looks, not 1. Subtracting a 1.0 baseline would drive every farm's "excess" large and negative, with the ordering dominated by the looks estimate rather than heterogeneity.

That is precisely the failure Megalodon documented and retracted: "a per-pixel estimate against a farm-level statistic drove most farms negative." Adopting Orion's formulation verbatim would have introduced the exact bug another team had already published a retraction about.

09The geocoding residual, finally measured

The last borrowed claim whose premise we had never tested. It turns out to be false for us — and the fix is still worth doing, for a different reason.

prep_r2.geocode() calls reproject(gcps=...), and GDAL with 225 GCPs fits an order-3 polynomial — the class Coding Bits measured at ~8 m and replaced with a spline.

Method note. Scoring a spline by its residual at the control points would be rigged — a TPS interpolates every one exactly, so its error there is zero by construction. Both are scored leave-one-out, which is what matters for a farm boundary sitting between control points.

Verdict — three parts, none of them their argument

1. The premise does not transfer. Their polynomial residual was ~8 m; ours is 3.6 m median — inside one 5 m base cell, 7% of our 52 m plot side. Our fit is about twice as good as the one they rejected, so "polynomial geocoding is a defect" is not a claim we can inherit.

2. But the spline still halves it, 3.62 → 1.78 m median, 18.5 → 15.2 m at p95. The tail is where it bites: a 19 m error is 35% of a plot side — real boundary mixing on the worst-placed farms, with erosion as our only current guard.

3. The cheapest win isn't the spline. Forcing order 2 recovers 0.46 m for a one-argument change, on every date, because GDAL's automatic order-3 selection is overfitting our GCP lattice. A cubic is worse than a quadratic here on held-out points.

Caveat. This measures the GCP fit only. It cannot see a common-mode error shifting every GCP equally — the class Megalodon's geoid bug belonged to, and exactly the class our own calibration error belonged to. Absolute registration against the vendor's geocoded product is a separate, still-unmeasured question.

12Round 3 preparation

What the shortlist reveals about the scoring

All six shortlisted writeups share five traits. That is not coincidence — it is the scoring function:

  1. Reported negative results. Megalodon retracted a published finding; Orion reports a component of its own index as null; we report three failures, one of which changed a shipped column.
  2. Validation against something the pipeline never read. Universal.
  3. Explicit reproducibility — a hash or an assertion. Universal.
  4. A stated scope limit on X-band — every team says some version of "relative within crop, not absolute biophysical retrieval".
  5. Physically justified constants, not fitted ones.

The highest-value action outside the code

Consensus pseudo-ground-truth. Six teams labelled the same 966 farms. Where four or more agree, that is the closest thing to labels that will ever exist for Sokhda — and it would let us measure our crop column against something external for the first time.

Answered — see §05. All five competitor submissions were compared. There is no usable consensus: 4 farms of 966 carry unanimous agreement, and the majority label separates the withheld sensors worse than four of the six individual maps. The consensus reference does not exist, and that is the finding.

Other uses for this material

  • Convergent validity as evidence. Where independent teams derive the same constant it is corroborated; where we are the lone dissenter it is a defect. The calibration went against us, the crop mix went for us. Both belong in the Round 3 report.
  • A failure-mode library. Between them the five teams document a geoid bug, an FID off-by-one, a calibration double-correction, an SNR-proxy artefact and a speckle-filter-induced correlation. Every one survived internal consistency checks in a competent pipeline — and every one is a cheap check we can add.
  • Anchor reconciliation. Four teams, four different yield anchors and a 2.1× production spread. Orion's note that Indian statistics quote the same crop three ways — with their cotton lint→kapas conversion at 34% ginning outturn — is the standard to match.
