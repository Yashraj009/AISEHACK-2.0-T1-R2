TEST 1  CALIBRATION, ADJUDICATED BY THE VENDOR'S OWN NOISE FLOOR
==============================================================================
  The GEO preview cannot settle this: it is uint8, 0-255, a stretched quicklook
  with no radiometric meaning. (DeepThinkers compared against it and got an
  offset that reversed sign on one of four dates -- that is the reason why.)

  `collect.image.nesz_peak` is different: a vendor-declared ABSOLUTE dB level.
  Physics: a scene contains smooth dark surfaces whose return approaches the
  system noise floor, so the darkest percentiles must sit near it -- never tens
  of dB above, which would mean the sensor never reaches its own noise anywhere.

  date        NESZ dB  darkest 0.1%   vs NESZ    under SF^2   vs NESZ
  20250606     -26.13           0.0     +26.1         -26.7      -0.6
  20250619     -27.76          -1.5     +26.3         -27.7      +0.0
  20250814     -27.97          -1.4     +26.6         -28.4      -0.4
  20251013     -27.35           0.9     +28.2         -27.8      -0.4

  As shipped, the noise floor sits +26.8 dB above the declared NESZ.
  Under SF^2 it lands on it, mean absolute error 0.36 dB, on FOUR scenes
  with four different NESZ values and four different scale factors.

  VERDICT: the squared convention is confirmed on vendor metadata alone. No
  competitor claim, and no reading of the ESA note, is load-bearing here.

TEST 2  THE BAJRA ANCHOR -- is the field right, and for what reason?
==============================================================================
  2a. Is our own source table internally consistent?
     crop         implied kg/ha    stated      gap
     Cotton               4,472       776      n/a   production is in BALES, 829,400 x 170 kg = 141 kt
     Rice                 1,833     1,690    -7.8%
     Maize                2,231     2,312    +3.6%
     Bajra                2,478     2,714    +9.5%
     Groundnut            2,490     2,514    +1.0%

     The yield column is not derivable from the area and production columns
     beside it. Bajra carries the largest gap of the four non-cotton crops,
     +9.5%, in the direction that inflates our anchor. The table's own
     production/area gives bajra 2.478 t/ha, not the 2.714 we ship -- a 9% cut
     before any competitor number is consulted.

  2b. Is there a mechanism that explains the remaining gap?
     Ours 2.48-2.71 t/ha. Theirs: Orion 1.79, Megalodon 1.89, DeepThinkers 1.36.
     Still a factor of ~1.5 apart, so one side is measuring something else.

     Gujarat grows bajra in TWO seasons, and they are not comparable:
       summer, irrigated   4,000-5,000 kg/ha   (ICRISAT pearl millet manual)
       rainfed kharif      1,200-1,800 kg/ha   (12-18 quintals/ha)
       irrigated general   2,500-3,500 kg/ha

     Our anchor is an ANNUAL district figure -- every season summed. It lands at
     2.5-2.7 t/ha, exactly where a blend of high-yield summer bajra and lower
     rainfed kharif bajra should land. Every competitor figure is explicitly a
     KHARIF estimate, and all three sit inside the rainfed kharif band.

     Round 2 observes 6 Jun - 13 Oct 2025. That is kharif. The anchor must be a
     kharif anchor.

     VERDICT: the field is right, but not because three teams agree -- because
     our figure answers a different question than the one we asked it. This is
     a season-matching error in our own sourcing, and we can state it that way
     without citing anybody.

  2c. Which other anchors carry the same exposure?
     crop       2nd season in Gujarat?      exposure
     Bajra      summer, irrigated, 2-3x     HIGH -- annual mean is inflated
     Maize      rabi maize, higher yield    MODERATE -- same mechanism, smaller
     Rice       summer rice is minor        low
     Groundnut  ~90% kharif in Gujarat      low
     Cotton     kharif only                 none

     So the fix is not 'copy their bajra number'. It is: source a KHARIF anchor
     for every crop, and check each crop for a second cropping season before
     using an annual district mean. That rule catches bajra and flags maize,
     and it is derived from agronomy rather than from a competitor's table.
