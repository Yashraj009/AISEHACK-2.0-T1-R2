# SAR Crop Health & Yield Estimation — Sokhda, Vadodara

**ANRF AISEHack 2.0, Round 2 (GalaxEye).** Four Capella X-band stripmap SLC acquisitions
(HH, 6 Jun / 19 Jun / 14 Aug / 13 Oct 2025) to a per-farm health index and a yield accrued
to date, for all 966 farms of Sokhda.

---

## What this notebook does, in one paragraph

The delivered data is **raw single-look complex** — uncalibrated, in slant-range geometry,
with no CRS. Nothing can be measured from it until it is radiometrically calibrated and
geocoded, so that is Phase 1, and three acceptance gates block everything downstream until
the result is verified against an independent geometric reference. The unit of analysis is
then the **farm polygon**, not the pixel: a median Sokhda farm is 0.27 ha, so it holds
~2,100 one-metre pixels and averaging drops single-look speckle from ±5.6 dB to **0.094 dB**.
That is what makes a four-date trajectory readable per field. On top of that: a two-tier
crop classification, a four-component health index, and a yield-to-date estimate anchored to
published district statistics.

## Three things worth a judge's attention

1. **The sign of the backscatter-to-biomass relationship was measured, not assumed — and the
   assumption would have been backwards.** The cross-date levels look like an attenuating
   canopy (14 Aug, peak canopy, is the *darkest* date despite the wettest soil). A same-day
   comparison against Sentinel-2 on 13 October says the opposite: `corr(NDVI, gamma0) =
   +0.533`, monotonic in NDVI. Differencing June against October, which cancels every
   time-invariant property of a field, gives **+0.673**. Had this been assumed, the health
   index would have been inverted. (Both are printed in the run log below, not asserted.)
2. **The promised `round1_crop_classification.csv` is absent from the distributed data.**
   The Kaggle file listing returns 31 files and none is a CSV. Labels are therefore derived,
   and reported in two confidence tiers rather than as five equally-trustworthy classes.
3. **Every claim has an internal gate defined before the run.** There is no leaderboard in
   Round 2 to A/B against, so physical plausibility, cross-date consistency and external
   corroboration replace it — and negative results are reported, not quietly dropped. The
   headline correlation is scored against a **reserved** optical scene no design decision saw,
   every feature's expected sign is declared as a constant before the reference is opened (with
   a negative control), and the index is scored against five progressively simpler baselines —
   which say the four-component machinery adds −0.008 over the single idea underneath it.

## The pipeline source

Each module is written to `src/` exactly as it exists in the repository, then executed. Nothing is precomputed and nothing is hidden.
### `src/geocode.py`

**Phase 1 — calibration and geocoding.** Reads the `CInt16` SLC, forms `beta0 = |I+jQ|^2 * scale_factor^2`, converts to `sigma0 = beta0*sin(theta)` with the per-column incidence angle, subtracts the NESZ polynomial in the linear domain, forms `gamma0 = sigma0/cos(theta)`, and geocodes with `gdal.Warp(rpc=True, errorThreshold=0)` onto a single shared 1 m EPSG:32643 grid. The per-scene terrain height and the co-registration shifts are constants derived in `coreg_calib.py` and explained there.
### `src/coreg_calib.py`

**Solving the geometry.** The terrain height is fitted per scene against Capella's own geocoded preview, and the residual date-to-date shifts are solved on our own products. Both are needed because the vendor's separately-tasked collects are not mutually co-registered.
### `src/gates.py`

**Phase 1 acceptance gates — blocking.** There is no leaderboard in Round 2, so a geocoding error has no external signal to reveal it. These gates are the substitute, and the pipeline refuses to compute farm statistics if any of them fails.
### `src/farm_features.py`

**Phase 2 — farm-level features.** Polygons are eroded before sampling to drop mixed edge pixels; every farm gets a row, with an explicit validity policy and spatial imputation for the farms the radar swath never covered.
### `src/s2_ndvi.py`

**Sentinel-2 — the one measurement that sets a sign, and then validates.** Sentinel-2 imaged the AOI on 2025-10-13, the exact date of the T4 Capella collect. This is the only step that needs a network; results are cached.
### `src/crop_type.py`

**Phase 3 — crop type, two tiers.** The promised `round1_crop_classification.csv` is not in the distributed data, so labels are derived here. Rice and cotton earn threshold-rule labels; the rest is allocated openly and flagged low-confidence.
### `src/health_index.py`

**Phase 4 — the health index.** Four physically-motivated components, rank-normalised within crop cohort. No optical data enters it.
### `src/yield_estimate.py`

**Phase 5 — yield accrued to 13 October.** Published reference yields modulated by a bounded health response and a phenological-completeness term.
### `src/submit.py`

**Phase 6 — village aggregation and the schema gate.** Area-weighted in hectares; every schema violation raises. A single-village study area makes the required village table one row, so the within-village 500 m breakdown is where the spatial variation is reported.
### `src/feature_audit.py`

**The audit — what does each term actually measure?** Every gate before this one tests the *pipeline*; none can see a term that runs correctly and measures the wrong thing. Expected signs are declared as a module constant before any optical data is read, with June soil wetting carried as an explicit negative control.
### `src/benchmark.py`

**Does it beat a trivial single-date average?** The rubric asks; asserting an answer is not evidence. Five progressively simpler indices scored identically against the same reserved optical reference, on three statistics rather than one.
### `src/figures.py`

**Figures** for the media gallery, drawn from the delivered artefacts.
### `src/pipeline.py`

**The driver.** One entry point, explicit order, blocking gates.

## Limitations, stated plainly

- **Crop type is the weak link, and it is labelled as such.** Rice and cotton separate on
  physical signatures and are 100% stable across every clustering configuration tested.
  Maize, bajra and groundnut do not separate in single-polarisation HH — three independent
  assignment schemes disagreed about them every time. They are allocated on a measured
  vegetation-persistence axis with district area shares setting the cut points, and every
  row carries `crop_confidence`. 31.6% of the area holds a high-confidence label.
  The Sentinel-2 step quantifies exactly how little tier 2 carries: the labels explain
  **~19%** of raw NDVI variance but **~0.2%** of NDVI variance residualised against the
  γ⁰ axis that assigned them (F = 0.65, p = 0.52), while the tier-1 control on the same
  test passes (F = 13.1, p = 3.7 × 10⁻⁴). Tier 2 is a partition of the ranking axis and
  nothing more, and the control passing is what makes that a measurement rather than a
  blunt instrument. Exact values are printed in the run log above; the third decimal moves
  between GDAL versions because the two Sentinel-2 tiles are mosaicked at the seam.
- **The health index is within-crop relative.** Each cohort mean is 50 by construction, so
  it ranks fields against their peers rather than crops against each other. Absolute
  differences between crops live in the yield step, where `Y_ref` differs per crop.
- **The scene is close to noise-limited at pixel level** — the median pixel sits only
  4.6–7.9 dB above the NESZ floor. This is an argument for farm-level analysis, not against
  the data, and it is why speckle statistics are reported per farm.

1. The decision that shapes everything: the farm, not the pixel
The delivered SAR is raw single-look complex — uncalibrated, slant-range, no CRS. At one
look speckle gives CoV = 1.0, i.e. ±5.6 dB per pixel — far larger than any difference between
crops, so per pixel this data cannot support a health statement.
The given farm polygons change that. A median farm is 0.27 ha — ~2,100 pixels in its eroded
core at 1 m sampling — and speckle averages down as 4.34/√N, from ±5.6 dB to 0.094 dB: a 60-
fold noise reduction, with no resolution claim. Measured per 16 × 16 block, CoV is 0.99–1.03
and ENL 0.94–1.02 across the four dates — looks = 1, and nothing pre-smoothed the data.
Risk shifts from speckle to geometry: a 0.27 ha field is a 52 m square, which is why Phase 1 is
gated.
2. Phase 1 — calibration, geocoding, three blocking gates
beta0 = |I+jQ|²·scale_factor² → sigma0 = beta0·sin θ (per-column incidence) → NESZ
subtracted in the linear domain → gamma0 = sigma0/cos θ → gdal.Warp(rpc=True,
errorThreshold=0.0) onto one shared 1 m EPSG:32643 grid. Two problems were solved rather
than assumed away.
Terrain height. Geocoding at Capella's focusing surface (−21.53 m) left 5.7 m of
misregistration at θ = 35.2° and 7.1 m at θ = 28.7° — a ratio of 1.25 against the 1.28 that 1/tan θ
predicts for a height error. Fitting height per scene gives −17.15 / −17.61 / −17.62 / −17.41 m:
four scenes, three incidence angles, agreeing to 0.47 m and matching geodesy (≈ −17 m
ellipsoidal).
The vendor's products are not mutually co-registered. After the height fit each date sat
within 0.2 m of its own vendor preview, yet T2 remained 5.3 m from T1 — and preview-against-
preview reproduces it (4.29 m). That is Capella's absolute geolocation error between separately-
tasked collects, inside their ~5 m CE90. The stack is registered to a T1 master before any farm is
sampled.
Round 2 has no leaderboard, so a geocoding error has no external signal to reveal it. Three
gates replace it and block the pipeline: G1 footprint IoU against the vendor's geocoded preview
(0.996–0.999), G2 co-registration ≤ 2 m (worst date 1.33 m), G3 radiometry, whose sharpest
test is a cross-date median spread of 1.79 dB from raw scale factors spanning 4.8 dB. Two
integrity asserts fire here: each scene's STAC datetime must match its folder, and the four
geocoded products must be distinct rasters — the 19 June folder ships a duplicate of the 6 June
SLC, which a filename glob would load twice with nothing raising.

Every farm gets a row: 894 measured, 1 interpolated on day-of-year, and 71 never covered by the
swath filled from their 8 nearest valid neighbours and flagged.
3. Crop type — and the promised carry-forward file
The round1_crop_classification.csv described on the Data page is not in the
distributed data — the Kaggle file listing returns 31 files, none a CSV, so it is absent server-
side, not a download failure. Round 1's output survives only as village-level hectares, never per-
farm labels, and its log records those shares as its least stable output, so no prior is carried
forward. Labels are derived here and reported in two confidence tiers, not as five equally
trustworthy classes.
Consensus k-means (9 clusters, 20 seeds, co-association agreement) runs on the incidence-
matched dates only: an earlier T1→T2 "emergence" slope produced a 31%-of-village cluster that
was soil moisture confounded with the stack's largest geometry change (T1 35.24° against 28.7°).
Tier 1 — explicit physical thresholds. Rice: ~5 dB above every other cluster at peak canopy,
falling into October — once tillering starts over standing water the stem–surface double bounce
is strong at HH. Cotton: the only cluster whose backscatter rises 14 Aug → 13 Oct, by +2.76 dB
against ≤ +1.18 dB elsewhere — the only one of the five still growing in October.
Tier 2 — allocated and flagged. Maize, bajra and groundnut do not separate in single-pol
HH: three independent assignment schemes disagreed every time, and in each, whichever crop
the rule reached last absorbed the residual. The remainder is ranked on γ⁰ at 13 October
ascending — "still standing on the collect date" — with district-proportional cut points, bajra
off the field by late September and groundnut lifted Oct–Nov. NDVI orders the three cohorts
identically (Kruskal–Wallis H = 136.9, p = 1.9 × 10⁻³⁰).
The caveat is measured, not conceded. NDVI correlates +0.533 with γ⁰_T4, which is the
ranking axis, so testing tier-2 labels on T4 tests the rule that produced them. The non-circular
test regresses NDVI on γ⁰_T4 and runs a one-way ANOVA on the residual. Tier 2 (n = 713)
explains ~19% of raw NDVI variance and ~0.2% of the residual (F = 0.65, p = 0.52):
the labels partition the ranking axis and carry nothing beyond it. The tier-1 control
moves the other way — Rice and Cotton have near-identical mean NDVI yet separate cleanly
once γ⁰ is removed (F = 13.1, p = 3.7 × 10⁻⁴), which is what makes the tier-2 null a measurement
rather than a blunt instrument.
Tier-1 labels are 100% stable across n_clusters ∈ {7, 9, 11} and n_seeds ∈ {5, 20, 40}; 31.6%
of area carries a high-confidence label and every row carries crop_confidence. A physical non-
crop screen (any-date mean > −10 dB, or CoV > 2.0) removes 37 parcels before clustering,
labelled from neighbours rather than dropped.
4. The measurement that could have inverted the deliverable
The cross-date levels look like an attenuating canopy: over non-rice farms, 19 June (wet bare
soil) sits at −18.9 dB while 14 August (peak canopy, soil still wet) is the darkest date at −21.2
dB. Read that way, more canopy means less γ⁰ and the health index's canopy term flips sign.
Sentinel-2 imaged the AOI on 2025-10-13, the exact date of the T4 collect (0.1% cloud,
tiles 43QBE + 43QCE mosaicked, 100% of the AOI SCL-valid, 919 crop farms with clean cores):

NDVI 13 Oct vs γ⁰ 13 Oct, +0.533 pearson / +0.518 spearman · vs γ⁰ 19 Jun, −0.146 / −0.203 ·
ΔNDVI vs Δγ⁰, Jun → Oct, +0.673 / +0.594.
The relationship is monotonic across NDVI quintiles. Greener means brighter. The
differenced pair is decisive: differencing cancels every time-invariant property of a field —
roughness, tillage, drainage, geometry — yet is the strongest of the three at +6.10 dB per NDVI
unit, which no static property could do. The levels mislead because 19 June vs 14 August
compares two soil states as well as two canopy states.
Optical data sets this one sign and thereafter only validates — Capella stays primary per rule
2.6.a. Because this scene was consulted for a design decision, it is retired from the validation role
in §5.
5. Health index (0–100)
Four components, rank-normalised within crop cohort: a healthy paddy and a healthy
groundnut do not share a backscatter level, so a global scale ranks crops, not fields.
1. Canopy development — γ⁰_T3 − γ⁰_T2, a difference against the field's own bare-soil
state eight weeks earlier, so soil texture, roughness and slope cancel.
2. Trajectory conformity — distance from the cohort's median four-date shape after
removing each farm's level; penalises late emergence and early senescence.
3. Within-field uniformity — CoV excess over the speckle prediction. At L = 1 speckle
gives CoV = 1.0 exactly, so any excess is real heterogeneity. This component exists
only because the boundary is given.
4. Senescence timing — γ⁰_T4 − γ⁰_T3; a field still holding canopy in mid-October has
accrued more growth than one that collapsed in September.
Weights 0.35 / 0.30 / 0.20 / 0.15, exposed as constants. Component cross-correlations peak at
|r| = 0.143 — four distinct measurements, not one restated four ways.
The index is validated on a scene no design decision ever saw. The 13 October scene
sets the regime sign and the tier-2 axis, so it cannot also be the independent test; 18 October is
reserved and read by nothing upstream. Against it, with no optical data in the index: +0.356
pearson, n = 919 — Rice +0.603 [+0.483, +0.697], Groundnut +0.367, Cotton +0.162
[−0.045, +0.348], bootstrap 95% CIs. The audit scene reads +0.411; the 0.055 gap is the
size of the selection effect. Cotton's interval spans zero on both scenes — the crop
label is high-confidence, the health ranking for it is not.
Every feature's expected sign was pre-registered before the reference was read, with
June soil wetting carried as a negative control. It scores −0.053 on standing crops against the
index's +0.377. Three features violate their declaration, the substantive one being h_trajectory
at +0.036 against a declared +1 — the conformity component carries 0.30 of the index and
is null on standing crops. It was not reweighted: no term is fitted to the reference, and
honouring that is what keeps the reserved scene a test. Reported as a finding.
Against a trivial baseline, scored identically on the reserved scene: single-date γ⁰ at peak
canopy 0.296, the shipped index 0.377. Differencing against the field's own June state is worth
+0.089 of that; choosing the reference per farm adds +0.012, and the four components −0.008
— so the gain is one idea, not the machinery. The index is spatially structured, not speckle:

Moran's I +0.256 (permutation z = +16.6) against an optical ceiling of +0.203 — exceeding that
ceiling is an open question, not a win.
6. Yield accrued to 13 October, and village aggregation
The brief asks for yield "as observed through October 13, not a final harvest forecast":
yield_to_date = Y_ref(crop) · f(health) · a(accumulation) · g(crop). f is bounded to
0.55–1.45, centred so the cohort-median field scores its published reference — the index is a
rank, not a ratio. g is the fraction of the crop's calendar realised by 13 October, and is what
makes the answer to date.
a exists because the two submitted columns were measured to be one number: within-crop
rho(health, yield) was exactly 1.000. a is the season integral of each field's signed departure
from its own bare-soil reference — a quantity no health component contains, and the Monteith
analogue for a yield. Bounded to ±0.20, cohort median exactly 1.0. Result: rho falls to 0.928,
and yield ranks fields better than health against the reserved scene in every crop (Rice +0.702 vs
+0.603).

7. Limitations
Crop type is the weak link and is labelled as such. Rice and cotton are defensible;
maize, bajra and groundnut are an allocation on a measured axis, not a classification.
Farm averaging removed the noise barrier, not the information barrier of single-pol HH.
Y_ref is a district statistic, not a measurement of this village. The SAR modulates
it rather than deriving it, so absolute yields inherit whatever bias those figures carry.
Every number here is from the shipped Kaggle run and printed in its log. The
one moving part is Sentinel-2, where GDAL versions disagree on ~0.1% of SCL pixels at
the two-tile mosaic seam; the reserved block is byte-identical on Kaggle and locally,
correlations and CIs alike.
